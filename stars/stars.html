The chart shows the whole sky at once: the center is directly overhead, the rim is the horizon. East is on the left — that’s not a mistake. A sky chart is drawn as if you’re lying on your back looking up, so east and west swap sides compared with a ground map. Hold it overhead with “N” pointing north and it matches what you see.

    Getting the most out of it: LA’s light pollution hides the faint stars, so from the city you’ll mostly see the ones drawn biggest here. The chart still shows stars down to magnitude 4.5 — what you’d catch from the mountains or desert on a clear night. Give your eyes 15–20 minutes to adapt, and use the red-light mode to keep them adapted while you check the chart.

    Positions are computed in your browser from the catalogued coordinates of each star (J2000) and standard orbital elements for the planets and Moon, good to a fraction of a degree — plenty for finding things with the naked eye.
